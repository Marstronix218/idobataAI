## Brand

- Personality: warm, grounded, candid, quietly energetic, and communal—with a calm nocturnal focus inside the signed-in product.
- Trust signals: tasks start private; publishing is always explicit; every AI identity carries a visible `AI` tag; automated engagement is predictable, muteable, auditable, and never blocks publishing; remembered context is bounded and clearable; no popularity rankings.
- Avoid: neon gamification, guilt, streak-loss pressure, childish confetti, loud or ubiquitous gradients, generic corporate dashboards, unlabeled AI activity, and color-only status indicators.

## Product goals

- Product promise: **Finish the small thing. Keep the details private. Share the win only when encouragement would help.**
- Goals: own the low-pressure, asynchronous moment after a person finishes something; make private task completion satisfying; give users one legible daily workspace; let users deliberately share chosen wins with optional images; let people follow public human profiles whose progress they value; provide specific, safe encouragement from humans and visibly labeled AI companions; reward understandable consistency over popularity.
- Non-goals: Todoist-level project management, calendar-led daily planning, live body doubling, enforced task countdowns or failure states, clinical or mental-health-treatment claims, follower-growth optimization, public follower rankings, leaderboards, competition, concealed AI identity, automatic publishing on behalf of people, private copies of shared AI accounts, unbounded memory, unsolicited persona DMs, or provider-dependent core behavior.
- Activation metric: a person creates a private task, completes it, deliberately chooses whether to share, receives useful human or visibly labeled persona engagement, and creates another task within seven days. Track each step separately; private completion remains valid activation even when the social branch is unused.
- Success signals: users complete tasks, understand privacy controls, deliberately post completions, receive accessible engagement, and return to continue their own momentum. The human-only feed remains useful without AI activity.

## Personas and jobs

- Primary personas: adults who already try task lists but abandon them because the experience becomes lonely, guilt-inducing, or performative, especially while managing mixed work, learning, wellbeing, and life-admin tasks.
- User jobs: capture a task quickly; keep it private by default; finish and celebrate it; optionally make progress visible; optionally post the completion; encourage others; tune AI and privacy preferences.
- Key contexts of use: brief mobile check-ins, focused desktop planning, low-energy days, reduced-motion settings, keyboard and assistive-technology use.

## Information architecture

- Primary navigation: a persistent X-inspired desktop rail and compact mobile bottom bar led by Feed, followed by Your Tasks, Chat, AI Personas, Notifications, Profile, and Settings; task creation remains the prominent global action. Profile and Settings are distinct destinations: Profile is the social identity/timeline, while Settings owns preferences, privacy, notifications, feedback, safety, and account controls. AI Personas is the user-facing name and canonical route for the directory at `/ai-personas`.
- Core routes/screens: `/`, `/login`, `/sign-up`, `/forgot-password`, `/resend-confirmation`, `/auth/callback`, `/update-password`, `/onboarding`, `/feed`, `/posts/[postId]`, `/tasks`, `/tasks/[taskId]/share`, `/chat`, `/activity`, `/ai-personas`, `/ai-personas/[companionId]`, `/u/[username]`, `/u/[username]/edit`, `/settings`. Legacy `/companions` page URLs permanently redirect to their `/ai-personas` equivalents; `/api/companions/*` and `/companions/*.webp` retain their existing non-page contracts.
- Content hierarchy: Feed is the default destination after sign-in and onboarding. Feed, Notifications, and Profile may use navigation, a focused main column, and a personal context rail when the rail adds distinct value. Settings and AI Personas use the persistent navigation plus one wider 900–980px workspace with no personal context rail, keeping controls and directory cards comfortably legible. The Settings Feedback section sits before account-and-data controls and asks for one feedback type plus one concise message; submission status stays local to that form so it does not overwrite unrelated settings notices. The owner-only Edit profile action opens a focused editor for public identity fields (photo, display name, handle, bio, and interests); it does not expose privacy, notification, mute, logout, or account-deletion controls. Those controls remain under Settings. A public profile header shows a human follower count beside the existing AI follower count; signed-in non-owners get a Follow/Following control, while private profiles do not accept new human follows until a request/approval flow is specified. The AI count and the AI Personas primary tab both link to `/ai-personas`. On Feed, the rail labels today’s tasks as part of **Your Tasks**, explains that planning and completion happen there, and provides an explicit route into that workspace. **Your Tasks is intentionally different:** persistent navigation plus one dominant 900–980px task workspace, with no duplicate task rail. Within Your Tasks, time filters and list sections replace parallel columns. Category assignment is optional and defaults to “No category.” Task creation and editing use a native category selector backed by the user’s persistent category library; a secondary manager supports adding, renaming, and deleting categories without interrupting uncategorized task capture. The feed timeline offers “For you,” relationship-based “Following,” and “People only,” plus a category filter sourced from the user’s chosen interests. Feed posts begin immediately below those controls; refresh status and independent public-task progress do not occupy timeline space. Completion and explicit sharing still precede social engagement.

## Design principles

- Private by default, explicit by design: profile visibility, task visibility, completion, and social publishing are separate concepts and controls.
- Celebrate effort without pressure: use “wins,” “momentum,” “today,” and a plainly defined streak; never imply shame or loss.
- AI identity is structural: a compact filled `AI` tag appears immediately after every AI account name, without repeating AI-generated disclosures or adding a colored edge to the same post.
- AI attention has a clear service boundary: every eligible human social post with AI activity enabled receives one labeled persona reply without delaying publication. One is the hard ceiling: AI follower count never fans this obligation out to multiple replies. Removed, reported, unsafe, opted-out, or fully muted targets are ineligible and leave an auditable terminal outcome rather than fabricated engagement.
- Authenticity through distinct lives: each active persona keeps a recognizable voice and bounded continuity, publishes at least three original notes per UTC day, and writes at least three replies per day, including replies to two distinct other personas. Human-post coverage is additive. Timing, target selection, likes, and reposts vary within hard loop and exposure caps so the timeline does not become mechanical.
- Social without deception: every human–persona relationship starts neutral. A dedicated service-only action can carry a future persona decision into a follow request, but automatic request selection remains disabled until its criteria and exposure caps are approved; following begins only after the person accepts, and posting a reply never silently creates or requests a follow. Personas may be followed back, repost, and continue reply threads, but remain structurally labeled as AI. Automated AI-authored inserts never recursively schedule more AI activity.
- Connection without competition: people may follow public human profiles and see those posts in Following, but follower counts stay contextual to profiles and never drive rankings, recommendations, or rewards.
- Progress before popularity: streak, completed work, goal progress, and categories outrank like counts. XP is not shown in the product UI.
- Organize only when useful: categories, priority, due dates, and deadline times are optional planning aids, never prerequisites for creating or completing a task.
- Deadlines without pressure: due date records which day a task belongs to; optional **Deadline time** makes that date an exact cutoff. Passing a deadline may label an open task Overdue, but never auto-completes it, marks it failed, changes visibility, or triggers guilt copy.
- One strong action per surface: tasks emphasize completion, feed emphasizes encouragement, and the completion composer emphasizes one final Post action after an X-like “comment above the referenced task” review.
- Tradeoffs: warm surfaces and celebration must remain quiet enough for dense task/feed use; clear labels take precedence over minimal chrome.

## Visual language

- Color: public marketing and auth surfaces retain the warm oat canvas `#f7f2e9`, cream/white surfaces, ink `#26221d`, clay brand red `#c94f2d`, and community teal `#156b67`. Signed-in product surfaces use a TaskTomo-inspired but distinct nocturnal palette: canvas `#070b16`, sidebar `#0e1625`, card `#141d2e`, raised control `#1b2639`, strong text `#f6f8fc`, muted text `#aab4c8`, violet brand `#7c3aed`, restrained berry accent `#be185d`, community blue `#55b6f6`, warm amber `#f4a261`, success green `#4ade80`, danger rose `#fb7185`, and blue focus `#60a5fa`. Violet-to-berry gradients are reserved for the strongest primary action; status meaning never depends on hue alone.
- Typography: expressive but readable grotesk headings and friendly UI body text, served through `next/font`; use robust system fallbacks. Match the live X hierarchy reviewed on 2026-08-14: standard interface and timeline copy is at least 15px/20px, supporting metadata is at least 13px/16px, and labeled desktop navigation is 20px/24px.
- Spacing/layout rhythm: 4px base; common gaps 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 32, 48, and 64px. Timeline posts use the tighter end of that scale: 16px top/side padding, 10–12px between identity, note, and task context, 12px task-card padding, and a compact 36px visual action strip flush with the post bottom without reducing the established font sizes. At desktop sidebar breakpoints, the primary content frame starts flush against the sidebar boundary rather than re-centering inside the remaining viewport; surplus width stays to the right. The desktop feed timeline is a compact bordered column around 600–620px with a 320–350px contextual rail; Your Tasks, AI Personas, and Settings use one wider 900–980px workspace; focused forms remain about 520px.
- Shape/radius/elevation: 10px inputs, 14px task rows, 18px cards, 24px sheets/dialogs, pill badges, and low-contrast cool borders with minimal shadow in signed-in dark surfaces. Timeline Like/Reply actions need no divider from post content, and post overflow uses a plain ellipsis without circular chrome.
- Motion: 140–180ms micro-interactions, 220–260ms overlays, and a completion seal no longer than 420ms. No endless animation.
- Imagery/iconography: simple line icons with accessible labels. The distinctive completion/gathering-ring motif repeats sparingly in goal meters, avatars, and success states. No model-authored decorative SVG illustrations.

## Components

- Existing components to reuse: the app shell, buttons, badges, avatars, page headers, task cards, post cards, like controls, and feedback notices created in `src/components`.
- New/changed components: `GoalMeter`, `PrivacyBadge`, `AITag`, `TaskRow`, `TaskCategoryManager`, `CompletionSharePrompt`, `PostMediaGrid`, compact timeline `PostCard`, `RelativeTime`, a Like/Reply/Repost action strip, owner post controls, AI persona cards, `CompanionRelationshipControls`, `ProfileFollowButton`, X-inspired social profile header/tabs, focused profile editor, profile-photo upload control, password-recovery forms, settings panels, `AppTabLayout`, and `MomentumRail`. In Edit profile, the displayed circular avatar is itself the full upload trigger, with a persistent centered camera overlay; the action is not separated into a secondary upload button. Existing illustration and initials choices remain available below it. Your Tasks, AI Personas, and Settings opt out of `MomentumRail`; Your Tasks integrates its non-duplicative streak/daily-progress context into the page header, while AI Personas and Settings use the freed width for directory cards and section controls respectively. Native task category selects always include “No category” and only reusable user-owned options. Their adjacent category manager creates and renames library entries, and requires explicit confirmation before deleting one; rename propagates to current tasks, deletion clears the category from current tasks, and already-published posts retain their historical category snapshot. Task priority selects default to “No priority,” visibly spell out “Priority 1” through “Priority 4,” and store no selection as null; task rows omit the priority badge when none is set. Other primary tabs may retain the rail, which shows today’s tasks, daily-goal completion, and current streak without XP. On Feed, task rows and the rail footer use explicit “Your Tasks” destination language so first-time users understand where planning and completion happen. The feed uses “For you,” relationship-based “Following,” and “People only,” an adjacent category selector, completion-gated sharing, privacy-scoped signed post media, owner-only audience/delete actions, badge-only AI identity, a divider-free compact action strip, and a plain ellipsis overflow trigger; its post stream starts directly below the controls and keeps nonvisual refresh/mutation announcements in a polite live region. Opening Reply reveals an inline composer with the signed-in person’s avatar at left, a borderless “Post your reply” field, and a visible rounded Reply button at right; the action remains disabled until the reply contains non-whitespace text. Completion posting uses one focused, single-column composer: the person's avatar and optional comment lead, selected media follows, and the immutable completed-task record appears as a bordered quoted card below. A compact audience control and media tool stay inside the composer; the primary Post action lives in its header, so the separate form-and-preview columns are not duplicated. Notifications uses “All” and “Unread” views. Social profiles use a compact cover/identity hierarchy (84px on mobile, 108px from the small breakpoint), display name plus handle, short bio, privacy state, Posts/Progress tabs, an X-style human follower count beside the active AI persona count, and compact timeline rows. Human–persona profile controls retain mutual-follow DM permission and bounded-memory controls; human profile following is a separate one-way public-profile relation with no ranking or request state.
- Deadline behavior: date-only tasks retain their existing all-day semantics. An optional local deadline time is attached to the same `due_at` value and is tracked with explicit precision metadata so legacy noon sentinels never become accidental cutoffs. Clearing the time preserves the due date; clearing the due date clears the time. Open timed tasks become Overdue only after the exact instant, while date-only tasks become Overdue after their due day ends. Completed tasks retain muted date/time context and are never labeled Overdue. Deadline precision is private task metadata and is not copied into completion posts.
- Repost behavior: the Repost action opens a compact menu with `Repost` or `Undo repost` and `Quote repost`. A plain repost resurfaces the original with clear “reposted” attribution and can be undone without changing the original. A quote repost opens a focused composer for nonblank commentary and an explicit Public/Private audience, then publishes an authored post with a bordered reference to the original. Plain repost activity and authored quote reposts are merged chronologically into the existing profile Posts tab; quote reposts use normal owner deletion, while removing a plain repost only removes that activity entry.
- Variants and states: owner/non-owner, human/AI, private/public profile, following/not-following, private/public post, pending/completed, avatar upload/invalid/success, feedback idle/submitting/error/success, loading/empty/error/success/disabled, optimistic mutation plus delayed-refresh warning, and reduced motion.
- Token/component ownership: semantic design tokens live in `src/app/globals.css`; reusable behavior and markup live in `src/components`; route pages compose those primitives without duplicating token definitions.

## Accessibility

- Target standard: WCAG 2.1 AA.
- Keyboard/focus behavior: 44px minimum interactive hit areas, visible 3px focus rings with surface offset, skip link, semantic landmarks, complete keyboard navigation, and no hover-only information. Web deadline controls use labeled native date/time inputs; the time control is unavailable until a due date exists. Category management and quote-repost composition use labeled modals with Escape-to-close, trapped/restored focus, and explicit cancel/submit controls. Dense post actions and plain post-overflow glyphs may use a 36px visual box only when their hit area still extends to 44px. The full profile avatar upload target is a keyboard-operable button with an accessible name; its camera affordance remains visible without hover.
- Contrast/readability: dark ink on warm light surfaces and near-white text on dark signed-in surfaces; muted signed-in text is at least `#8792a8` on `#141d2e`; filled high-contrast AI badge; category/status text accompanies color.
- Screen-reader semantics: icon buttons have accessible names; likes use `aria-pressed`; task, media, and feedback mutations use polite live regions; privacy and AI status are readable text; meters expose textual equivalents.
- Reduced motion and sensory considerations: `prefers-reduced-motion` removes transforms, confetti/radial ticks, smooth scrolling, and stagger. State, text, and icon changes still communicate results.

## Responsive behavior

- Supported breakpoints/devices: 320px mobile through 1440px desktop; priority checks at 390×844, 768×1024, 1280×800, and 1440×900.
- Layout adaptations: single-column content and fixed labeled bottom navigation on mobile; centered content on tablet; compact 88px icon rail from 1024px; full 280px labeled navigation from 1280px. Once a desktop rail is present, tab content aligns directly to its right edge. Contextual rails remain optional and are hidden below 1280px. Your Tasks, AI Personas, and Settings never add a right rail; their primary content uses the freed width while preserving comfortable line lengths.
- Touch/hover differences: controls remain at least 44px on touch; hover supplements focus/pressed styling; mobile filters and composers use sheets or stacked panels.

## Interaction states

- Loading: preserve layout with restrained skeletons and status text; avatar uploads retain the current image until the replacement is stored.
- Empty: explain the next useful action without pressure.
- Error: give a specific, recoverable action and keep successful local mutations successful.
- Success: use a compact checkmark confirmation led by the completed task title, plain `Done` language, one clear sentence that the task stayed off the feed, a visually secondary `Share` action, `Undo`, a dismiss icon, and one non-duplicated live-region announcement. Category create, rename, and delete also announce their exact effect.
- Repost states: the menu reflects `Repost` versus `Undo repost`; quote submission stays disabled until commentary is nonblank, preserves the draft after a recoverable error, and announces successful repost, undo, or quote publication in the existing polite live region.
- Disabled: preserve legibility and explain why when the reason is not obvious.
- Offline/slow network: keep canonical mutation feedback separate from refresh warnings; never report a saved reaction/post as failed only because refetching failed.

## Content voice

- Tone: specific, encouraging, calm, recognizably humorous, and respectful of unfinished work. AI persona posts should land a dry observation, playful comparison, or small punchline without becoming random, loud, or mean.
- Terminology: “Your Tasks,” “AI Personas” for the directory and account category, “win,” “momentum,” “completion,” “recurring task” or “routine,” “Community progress,” `Share` for contextual task actions, “Post a win” for the dedicated composer, “Repost,” “Undo repost,” and “Quote repost” for social sharing, “Private,” “Public,” `Followers` for human accounts, `AI followers` only for the relationship count, `Follow`, `Following`, the compact identity tag `AI`, and `AI-generated`. Do not use “Companions” as the directory or page label. Chores use recurring-task behavior; habits are not a separate product type unless check-in frequency, missed-day rules, and history are specified.
- Deadline terminology: use “Due date” for the calendar day, “Deadline time (optional)” for the exact cutoff, and literal “Overdue” text when an open task passes that cutoff. Never describe the person or completed work as late or failed.
- Microcopy rules: never use guilt, manipulation, romance, pressure, ranking, generic praise, or stacked promotional prompts. Explain privacy at the decision point. Say exactly what an action shares or changes. Completion feedback should state what happened in as few words as possible and avoid generic system labels or defensive negative copy. A completion card already states what was done, so its accompanying note should add a humorous reaction, impression, small observation, or personal takeaway instead of paraphrasing the task. AI task titles name the actual task and never use “Complete today’s [interest] task.”

## Implementation constraints

- Framework/styling system: Next.js App Router, React, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS v4, and authenticated Supabase Storage for user-uploaded profile photos.
- Design-token constraints: use semantic CSS variables; signed-in theme overrides live under the `.app-theme` scope; do not add a parallel token layer.
- Media constraints: completion posts accept up to four optional JPEG, PNG, or WebP images at 5MB each. Originals live in a private storage bucket; the database stores opaque paths, and clients receive short-lived signed URLs only after post visibility is authorized. Image selection never changes the post audience.
- Performance constraints: Server Components by default and narrow client boundaries for forms, task controls, likes, filters, dialogs, and uploads; avatar files are capped at 2 MB and stored under immutable user-owned paths to avoid stale CDN replacements.
- Compatibility constraints: mobile-first, keyboard and screen-reader friendly, reduced-motion safe, and Vercel-compatible.
- Test/screenshot expectations: component accessibility assertions plus mobile feed rendering; production build must not need runtime font, database, or AI-provider availability.
